## Decision points

### D1 — Drawing the promise: the symbol for "her offspring" and the Rescuer (p11, p13, p15, p19, p27, p29, p31; cover)
No figure, no halo, no baby (§2). The brief asks for ONE proposed primary symbol as the book's
visual refrain, linked to the sprouting seed in the mended vessel on the cover (R3).

**PROPOSED primary symbol: the seed-and-shoot** — a seed half-buried in a shallow furrow with
one green shoot and two simple leaves. Why it is proposed (facts, not a ruling): the Hebrew of
3:15 is *zeraʿ*, "seed", which WEB renders "offspring"; Gal 3:16 reads the promise as one
Seed; the cover (R3) already fixes "a single green shoot grows from soil inside" the mended
vessel, so the interior refrain and the cover are the same object. The shoot first appears
where the serpent passed (p11), stands alone as the refrain (p13), is beside Eve at the naming
(p15), is carried into the dark-to-dawn page (p19), appears inside the mended cup (p27, p29)
and closes the book a little taller (p31). It must stay a seedling — never a tree (the tree of
life is Book Three's distant F10 tree and must not be confused with it), never glowing.

The other candidates, with trade-offs:
- **Dawn light** (the sun as a half-circle on the horizon with short straight light-lines —
  Book Three's gate-light vocabulary): strongest "He lit a light" lyric match (lines 169-170);
  risk — light is also the Book Six+ vocabulary for Christ (§1) and in a coloring page can only
  be lines, so it must be proven at Stage 2 to read in pure black and white without grey. In the
  baseline it is the SECONDARY symbol (p13, p19, p21, p27, p29, p31), always from the horizon,
  never from a figure or the vessel.
- **A lit path from the closed gate:** reads "a way forward" clearly; risk — a path implies a
  destination and a walker, and a path with a figure on it is a §2 defect waiting to happen;
  Book Three banned "path" as 3:15 vocabulary. Not in the baseline; may be ruled in for p31.
- **A word-scroll:** matches "spoken word" and "through His Word and promises" (line 171);
  risk — anachronistic in Eden, and a scroll with no writing is an empty object for a child.
- **Seed in soil alone (no shoot):** truest to "not yet"; risk — a seed in the ground is almost
  invisible as a coloring object at page scale and does not rhyme with the cover's shoot.

**Ruling:** Seed-and-shoot is the primary symbol and the book's visual refrain (editor, in chat 2026-10-16). Dawn light stays the secondary symbol as drawn in the baseline, subject to the Stage 2 black-and-white proof.

### D2 — The serpent's head and the bruised heel (3:15b; p11)
How to show the promised victory without violence, without a crushing scene, and without a
human figure of the one who will do it.

- **Option A — Retreat and diminish, plus the seed (baseline):** the serpent small and far,
  slipping beneath the tree line with its head low; the shoot rising where it passed. The
  picture says "its day is ending; something new is coming". Trade-off: "bruise your head" is
  not drawn at all; the verse carries it.
- **Option B — Text only:** p11 prints 3:15b and shows the serpent exactly as p9 (no shoot);
  the victory is in the words alone. Trade-off: nothing on the page for the child to hold on
  to; p11 and p9 look alike.
- **Option C — The serpent's head down in the dust, a stone beside it (not on it):** closer to
  "head" and "dust" (3:14). Trade-off: a stone beside a snake invites "who threw it?" and a
  hand; one step from a crushing scene.
- **Option D — A heel-print in the soil (no foot, no figure):** names the heel. Trade-off: a
  footprint is a mark of a person, and §0 bans footprints standing for God; a child will ask
  whose it is — the honest answer is the one Book Six owns.
- **"Bruise his heel" (the promised one wounded)** is in every option a pointer for the
  teaching line, not a picture (F19).

**Ruling:** Option A — retreat and diminish, plus the seed (editor, in chat 2026-10-16). "Bruise his heel" is a pointer for the teaching line only.

### D3 — The serpent here versus Book Three (p7, p9, p11)
Same creature design as Book Three p5/p21 (no mouth line); never cute, demonic or human-like.

- **Option A — Diminished:** drawn smaller page by page (p7 mid-size, p9 smaller, p11 small and
  far). Trade-off: smallness can read as "harmless" rather than "cursed".
- **Option B — Retreating:** same size as Book Three, always moving away, head low, never
  facing the couple. Trade-off: strongest continuity; least change from p21 of Book Three.
- **Option C — Shadowed:** under the tree line, partly behind trunks or grass. Trade-off: in
  line art "shadow" is only occlusion — no grey, no fill — so it must be drawn as partly hidden,
  which can look like a snake hiding to strike.
- **Option D — Any two of A–C together** (the baseline uses A+B).
- Naming: Book Three F18 ruled "do not name Satan". Rom 16:20 names him (pointer only, F20).

**Ruling:** Option D as A+B — diminished AND retreating (editor, in chat 2026-10-16). Satan is not named (F20 carried from Book Three F18).

### D4 — Grace before merit (page order; p5, p7–p13 before p15–p17; p25 before anything the Son does)
The sequence must show God speaking the promise BEFORE any response from Adam and Eve, so no
child learns the promise was earned.

- **Sequence as planned:** called (p5) → cursed/promise (p7–p13) → Eve's naming and clothing
  (p15–p17) → sent out (p21) → family: breaking (p23), the Father comes (p25), mended (p27),
  "GOD DID" (p29). Trade-off: the family application sits late; the song's verse 1 ("when we
  ran, God stayed") is answered on p25, not p5.
- **Alternative — family application first (p3–p9), then Genesis:** matches the song order.
  Trade-off: the child sees "a parent stayed" before hearing "God promised", which is the
  reading R3 forbids (the picture outrunning the words).
- **D4b — Confession scene:** Book Three's amended D6/F16 said none before Book Seven.
  - **(i) None (continuity with F16):** the Son never kneels, folds hands or says sorry on any
    page; p23 is head-down alone, p25 is the Father arriving. Trade-off: the ordinary "owning
    it" moment a 4-year-old knows is not drawn.
  - **(ii) A child-level turning-toward (the Son looking up at the Father on p25):** no
    kneeling, no folded hands. Trade-off: can read as the trigger for the Father's coming —
    the Father must already be beside him before he looks up, or merit creeps back in.
  - **(iii) A confession page of its own:** Trade-off: breaks F16 and would need Book Seven's
    forgiveness vocabulary early.

**Ruling:** Sequence as planned (editor, in chat 2026-10-16). D4b (i): NO confession scene — the Son never kneels, folds hands or says sorry on any page; p25 is the Father arriving.

### D8 — Family-cast application under R3 (p23, p25, p27, p29; cover)
An ordinary breaking and an ordinary not-walking-away. The picture says "a parent stayed";
the author's words say "God promised".

**D8a — Where the mended vessel enters the interior.**
- **Option A — p27, the NEXT MORNING, received (baseline):** the cup stands on the step set
  back together, one seam line, the shoot inside; the children look at it; NOBODY holds or
  mends it; no glue, no tools, no hands on pieces. Who mended it is not shown — the words
  carry it. Trade-off: a child may ask "who fixed it?"; the honest answer is the teaching
  line's pointer (2 Cor 1:20: every promise of God is "Yes" in Him — sure, though not yet
  seen), and the picture must never answer "Dad did".
- **Option B — p29 only (the family page):** p27 stays with the cracked cup and the Father;
  the mended cup appears once, at the "GOD DID" page. Trade-off: one fewer picture of the
  promise; p27 and p25 look alike.
- **Option C — Cover only:** the interior never shows the mended cup; the shoot in the soil is
  the interior refrain, the cup-with-shoot is the cover's. Trade-off: the cover no longer
  "promises the interior" (cover-grid rule 6) for its central object.

**D8b — Who holds it.**
- **(i) Nobody (baseline):** the cup on the step; hands free (no contact pose — tube limbs,
  defects D-01/D-03).
- **(ii) The Son with both hands (mirrors the cover):** Trade-off: a two-hand hold is a contact
  pose; the cover solves it with a two-phase B&W reference, but a coloring page cannot be
  retouched the same way.
- **(iii) The Father hands it to the Son:** Trade-off: reads "Dad mended it" → R3 defect.

**D8c — Who mends it.** The brief asks "whether the Son or the Father does the mending".
Under R3 any human mending shown is a defect: the Son mending = "we fixed it"; the Father
mending = "the family fixed what sin broke". The only staging that keeps R3 is mending ALREADY
DONE and RECEIVED, with no mender on the page. This is reported as a constraint, not a ruling;
if the editor wants a mender shown, that is a change to R3.

**D8d — The breaking.**
- **(i) The same cup as Book Three (p19 crack; cover crack), in the backyard of the cover
  (baseline):** strongest continuity; the series says "this is the same brokenness".
- **(ii) The same cup indoors (Book Three p19's room):** Trade-off: interior continuity, cover
  continuity lost.
- **(iii) A new ordinary breaking:** Trade-off: the cover's mended cup then has no "before".

**D8e — Flags.** (1) Any page where the Father arrives AFTER the Son tidies, apologises or
picks up the pieces lets the child conclude staying was earned (D4). (2) Any page where a
family member holds glue, a tool or two pieces lets the child conclude the family fixed what
sin broke (R3). (3) The dog: present on the cover and p19/p25 of Book Three; present on
p25–p29 here; absent on p23 (the Son alone). (4) Six figures on p29 is the series' highest
cast count on one page — count failures (D-10) are the most likely defect.
