## Track 1

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.
I want to ask you a question. Are you ready? When you look around you for a second — at the sky, the ground, the trees outside your window. Have you ever wondered — who made all of this?
The Bible answers that question in the very first verse of the very first book. Genesis Chapter 1 verse 1.  It says, "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." Before there were mountains, before there were oceans, before there was one single bird singing one single song — there was God.
And here's what's amazing, friends. God did not need tools. He did not need helpers. He didn't need a hammer or a paintbrush or a whole team of builders. The Bible tells us God simply spoke. He said, in verse 3,  "Let there be light" — and there was light. He also said, "Let there be land and sea" — and it was so. Word by word, the whole world came alive, just because God said so.
Think about that. Who made the mountains, so tall and strong? God did. Who taught the tiny birds to sing their song? God did. Who made the wide, deep ocean, and the tallest tree, and the smallest flower? God did. And who made you? God did too — and he made you on purpose, because he loves you.
That's what makes God different from anyone or anything else in the whole universe. He's not just powerful — he's the King over everything he made. Big and small, near and far, it all belongs to him, and it's all good, because he is good.
So here's what I want you to do today, friends. The next time you see a mountain, or hear a bird, or watch the ocean, I want you to remember — that's not an accident. That's not just "nature." That is God, showing you a little piece of how great he is.
And when we remember that, what should we do? We should say thank you. Thank you, God, for the sun that warms us. Thank you, God, for the flowers and the trees. Thank you, God, for making the whole wide world — and for making me, too.
We made a song about exactly this. It's our very first track, and it is loud, and joyful, and true. Let's listen, sing and dance together.

## Track 2 — "Made Like Him"

Hi, friends. Always glad you're here.
Last time, we learned that God made everything — the mountains, the oceans, the birds, the trees. But today I want to tell you about the most amazing thing God ever made. And friends... it's you.
The Bible tells us something incredible in Genesis chapter 1, verse 27:
"So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them." (Genesis 1:27, ESV)
Now, that's a big verse, so let's think about what it means. When you look in a mirror, you see a reflection of yourself, right? Well, God made you and me to be like a reflection of him. Not the same as God — we're not gods — but made to show a little picture of who he is, everywhere we go. Isn’t that amzing?
Think about it. Because God is Love and he can love — he made you able to love too. God knows all things — and he made you able to think and learn and know. God can choose whatever He want’s to do — and he made you able to choose too. You were not made by accident. You were not a surprise. Before you were even born, God planned you, on purpose, to carry a little piece of his likeness into the world.
That means something important, friends. It means every single person you'll ever meet — your mom, your dad, your brother, your sister, your friend at school, even someone you've never met — red, yellow, black or white… they're made in God's image too. That's why we treat people with kindness. That's why every person matters. Because every person is carrying a little reflection of God.
So here's what I want you to remember today: you are not an accident. You are not ordinary. You are not a monkey, and you are not like your puppy! You are made like him — on purpose, for a purpose — to know God, to love others, and to shine a little bit of his glory everywhere you go.
We made a song about exactly this. It's our second track, and I think it's going to help you remember just how special you really are. Let's listen together.

## Track 3 — "We Broke It All"

Hi, friends. Always glad you're here.
So far we've talked about how God made everything good, and how he made you and me in his image, on purpose, to be like him. If that's true, here's a question worth asking: why do we sometimes see sad things in the world? Why do people fight? Do you fight sometimes? All the time? Why do we sometimes feel afraid, or see something get broken that can't be fixed?

The Bible has an honest answer, and I want to tell it to you straight, because it's true, and because you're ready to hear it. A long time ago, the first people God made — Adam and Eve — had a choice. God gave them one simple rule, and they said, "No, we'll do it our own way instead." They turned away from God. And that one choice broke something. Not just for them — for all of us. Here's how the Bible puts it, in Romans chapter 3, verse 23:
"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." (Romans 3:23, ESV)
Did you notice that word "all"? Not just Adam and Eve, long ago. All of us — you, me, everybody — we're all born with that same turning-away inside us. We call that sin. It's why we sometimes disobey and fight, even when we know better. It's why the world has sickness, and sadness, and things that just are not the way they're supposed to be.

Now, I know that might sound like a sad thing to talk about, and it is very sad. But friends, I don't want you to be afraid. Because sin is a real problem — but it's not the end of the story. Not even close.

Here's what I want you to hold onto today: God saw what happened, and God didn't just walk away and leave us in our mess. He already had a plan to fix what we broke. And that plan is so much bigger, and so much better, than the mess we made.

We made a song about this part of the story. It's honest about the hard part — but stick with it, because it ends with real hope. Let's listen and together.

## Track 4 — "He Didn't Walk Away"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

Last time, we talked about something hard — how we all turned away from God, and how that broke the world. If someone hurt your feelings and turned away from you, you might want to turn away from them too, right? That would make sense. So here's a question: when we turned away from God... did he turn away from us?

The Bible's answer is one of the most beautiful things you will ever hear: no. In fact, right after Adam and Eve disobeyed — right in that very same chapter — God made a promise. Genesis chapter 3, verse 15 says God spoke to the serpent, and said this:

"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel." (Genesis 3:15, ESV)

That's God promising, right there at the very beginning, that a Rescuer was coming — someone who would crush the power of sin once and for all. God didn't wait to see if we'd earn it back. He made the promise first, before we did anything to deserve it.

That's what grace looks like, friends. Grace means God loves us and helps us, not because we're good enough, but because he is good. Even when we mess up — even today, even this week — God doesn't walk away from the people who belong to him. He stays close. He keeps his promises. He is faithful, every single day, whether we feel it or not.

So here's what I want you to remember: no matter what you've done, no matter how far you feel from God today, he has not walked away from you. He's still writing this story, and it ends with a Rescuer, coming to bring us home.

We made a song about exactly this. It's our fourth track, and it's about a promise that never breaks. Let's listen together.

## Track 5 — "We Need a Hero"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

Let's play a quick game in our heads. Have you ever tried really, really hard to be good — and still messed up anyway? Maybe you tried not to be grumpy, and you got grumpy. Maybe you tried to share, and you didn't want to. I think all of us know that feeling. So here's today's big question: can we ever be good enough, all by ourselves, to fix the sin problem we talked about?

The Bible's answer might surprise you: no. Not even close. No matter how hard we try, we can't erase the wrong things we've done. We can't make ourselves perfect. Listen to how Romans chapter 5, verse 6 describes it:

"For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly." (Romans 5:6, ESV)

Did you catch that? It says "while we were still weak" — while we still couldn't save ourselves. We can't save ourselves — not one of us, not ever.

That might sound like bad news. But friends, this is actually the moment where the story gets exciting. Because if we can't rescue ourselves... we need someone else to rescue us. We need a hero. Not a hero with a cape and a mask — a real hero, with real power, who can do what we could never do on our own.

And here's the good news: God already knew we couldn't save ourselves. That's exactly why he promised a Rescuer back at the very beginning. He's not surprised that we need help. He planned for it, before the world even began.

So today, I don't want you to feel discouraged that you can't be perfect on your own. I want you to feel hopeful — because you don't have to be. There is a Hero coming, and he is good, and he is powerful, and he is exactly what we need.

We made a song about exactly this — about needing a hero, and about the hope of who's coming next. Let's listen together.

## Track 6 — "Jesus Is the One"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

We've been building up to this moment for a few tracks now. We learned God made everything good. We learned we broke it. We learned God didn't walk away, and that he promised a Rescuer. And last time, we learned we need a Hero, because we can't save ourselves. So here's today's question, and it's the biggest one yet: who is the Hero? Who is the Rescuer God promised?

Friends, his name is Jesus.

The Bible tells us Jesus is God's own Son. Before the world began, God planned to send him. And when the time was right, Jesus came — born as a baby, growing up just like you, but living a perfect life. Every single day, every single choice, Jesus obeyed God completely. He never sinned, not once. Here's how Romans chapter 5, verse 8 describes what he did next:

"But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." (Romans 5:8, ESV)

Jesus took our place on the cross. He took the punishment for OUR sin — yours and mine — even though he never did anything wrong. And right before he died, Jesus said, "It is finished" — meaning the rescue was complete.

But friends, that's not even the best part. Three days later, Jesus rose from the dead. He's alive! Death could not hold him down. He beat sin, and he beat death, and now Jesus is King over everything, forever.

So here's what I want you to remember: Jesus isn't just a nice story from long ago. He is real, he is alive, and he did all of this for you. He is the One.

We made a song about exactly this — about Jesus, our Rescuer, our Savior, our King. Let's listen together.

## Track 7 — "Grace, Grace, Grace"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

Here's a question I want you to really think about. If Jesus already rescued us — if he already lived the perfect life and took our punishment on the cross — what do WE have to do to be saved? Do we have to earn it? Do we have to be good enough first?

The Bible's answer is one word, and it's one of the most important words you will ever learn: grace. Listen to how Ephesians chapter 2, verses 8 and 9 describe it:

"For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast." (Ephesians 2:8–9, ESV)

Did you hear that? "Not your own doing." "The gift of God." "Not a result of works." That means we can't buy it. We can't earn it by being extra obedient. We don't get it by trying harder than everyone else. It's a gift — freely given, through Jesus. Even the faith to believe in Jesus is a gift from God, friends.

That means nobody gets to boast, "Look how good I am, that's why God loves me." Nobody. Every single person who's ever been rescued by Jesus was rescued the exact same way — by grace, and grace alone.

Isn't that good news? It means you don't have to be perfect to come to Jesus. It means on your best day and your worst day, God's love for you doesn't change, because it was never based on your performance in the first place. It's based on his grace.

So today, I want you to remember: you are loved, not because you earned it, but because God is gracious. And when we understand that, we don't get prideful — we get grateful. All the glory goes to him.

We made a song about exactly this. It's simple, it's joyful, and it says it three times so you never forget it: grace, grace, grace. Let's listen together.

## Track 8 — "I Am Brand New"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

We've talked about sin, and about Jesus rescuing us, and about grace being a gift. So here's today's question: when someone trusts in Jesus, what actually happens to them? Do they just get a little bit better? Or does something bigger happen?

Friends, the Bible says something happens that's bigger than "a little better." Listen to 2 Corinthians chapter 5, verse 17:

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come." (2 Corinthians 5:17, ESV)

A new creation. Not patched up — brand new. When we trust in Jesus, God gives us a brand new heart. The old, sin-loving heart doesn't get repaired — it gets replaced.

Here's what that means, step by step. God forgives us completely — that's called being justified. God starts changing us to become more like Jesus, day by day — that's called being sanctified. God adopts us into his own family, as his own children, forever — that's called being adopted. And one day, God will finish the work completely, and we will be fully like Jesus — that's called being glorified.

That's a lot of big words, friends, but here's the simple truth underneath all of them: if you belong to Jesus, you are not stuck being who you used to be. God isn't finished with you. He's making you new — new heart, new life, forever united to Jesus.

So even on the days you mess up — and you will, we all do — remember this: that's not the real you anymore. The real you is who God is making you into. Brand new, day by day, until the work is complete.

We made a song about exactly this — about being made brand new in Jesus. Let's listen together.

## Track 9 — "Follow Every Day"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

Here's a question for you. If we're saved by grace, not by being good enough — like we talked about last time — does that mean it doesn't matter how we live? Can we just do whatever we want now?

Friends, the Bible's answer is no — but not for the reason you might think. Listen to what Jesus himself said, in Luke chapter 9, verse 23:

"And he said to all, 'If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.'" (Luke 9:23, ESV)

Did you catch that word "daily"? Following Jesus isn't a one-time thing. It's every day. And here's the important part: we don't obey God to EARN his love. We already have that, as a free gift. We obey God BECAUSE we already love him, and because he already saved us. It's not "obey so God will love me." It's "God loves me, so I want to obey."

Following Jesus means walking with him every single day — not just on Sunday, not just when it's easy. Some days that means saying sorry when we've done wrong. Some days it means choosing kindness when it would be easier to be selfish. And here's the good news: we don't do this alone. God gives us his Holy Spirit to help us, every step of the way.

And when we do stumble — because we will — we don't have to hide it or pretend it didn't happen. We run straight to Jesus, say we're sorry, and start walking with him again. That's what following looks like. Not perfect. Just faithful, one day at a time.

So here's what I want you to remember: following Jesus isn't a one-time thing you did once. It's a whole life, walked one day at a time, with his help, all the way home.

We made a song about exactly this — about following Jesus, every single day. Let's listen together.

## Track 10 — "He Won't Let Go"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

Here's a question that might worry you a little, and I want to answer it honestly. If we still mess up sometimes — even after Jesus saves us — could we ever lose God's love? Could we mess up so badly that God finally gives up on us?

Friends, the Bible's answer is one of the most comforting truths you will ever hold onto. Listen to Romans chapter 8, verses 38 and 39:

"For I am sure that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 8:38–39, ESV)

Did you hear how many things that verse names — death, life, angels, powers, height, depth — and then says none of them can do it? Nothing can separate us from God's love in Christ. Not our mistakes. Not our fears. Not anything in this whole world.

Jesus himself is praying for us, right now, every day. And the same God who started this rescue in you is the same God who promises to finish it. He doesn't start something good and then walk away halfway through.

That doesn't mean we'll never struggle, or never feel weak, or never be afraid. It means when we do, God's grip on us doesn't loosen. His covenant — his promise — doesn't depend on how strong our grip is on him. It depends on how strong his grip is on us. And his grip never fails.

So if you're ever scared that you've messed up too much, or wandered too far, I want you to remember this: the Father chose you, Jesus saved you, and the Spirit sealed you, forever. That's not something you can undo. He won't let go.

We made a song about exactly this — about how safe we are in God's hands, forever. Let's listen together.

## Track 11 — "Forever and Ever"

Hi, friends. I'm so glad you're here with me today.

We've come a long way together, friends. We learned God made everything good, that we broke it, that God promised a Rescuer, that Jesus came and saved us by grace, that he makes us brand new, and that he'll never let us go. So today, for our very last question in this part of the story: how does it all end?

Friends, the Bible tells us this story doesn't end with sadness. Listen to what it says in Revelation chapter 21, verse 4:

"He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away." (Revelation 21:4, ESV)

Think about it: no more sickness. No more sadness. No more tears, no more pain, no more brokenness — ever again. Our own bodies will be raised, perfect and glorious, just like Jesus's. And best of all, we will see God face to face, and live with him forever, in a world that is finally, completely, the way it was always supposed to be.

That's why the very last chapter of the Bible ends with a prayer. Revelation chapter 22, verse 20 says:

"He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!" (Revelation 22:20, ESV)

We're not just waiting around, friends. We're waiting with hope, because we know how the story ends, and it ends with Jesus reigning forever, and his people worshiping him, forever and ever.

So here's what I want you to carry with you, friends, not just from today, but from everything we've learned together: this world is not as it should be yet — but it will be. Jesus is coming back. And when he does, everything sad will come untrue.

We made a song about exactly this — about the day Jesus comes back, and the forever that's waiting for us. Let's listen together.
