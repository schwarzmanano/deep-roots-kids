## Continuity carried from Book Two (facts, for checking)

- **Adam (made-like-him p7, p9, p11):** seen from behind or three-quarters behind, at a
  distance, face never shown. Short cropped hair, broader squarer build. A plain sleeveless
  tunic at or above the knee, one-shouldered on p7. Single-line limbs, plain oval hands and
  feet; one pointing gesture on p7.
- **Eve (made-like-him p7, p11):** long wavy hair past the shoulder, slighter build. A plain
  sleeveless knee-length dress, with a hem band on p7.
- **Garments are un-tailored:** no collar, tie, pattern or fur texture. Book Two D2 kept
  them plain "so they never read as Gen 3:21's garments of skin" (relevant to D3/D4).
- **Eden as drawn in Book Two:** rounded trees, a river, rolling hills. **p11 shows apple and
  pear trees** (relevant to D2). No central or special tree anywhere in Book Two (D12).
- **Book Two D12 deferred the Gen 2:16-17 command and the tree of knowledge to Book Three**
  (relevant to F1).

## Decision points

### D1 — The serpent (3:1 "more subtle than any animal of the field"; p5, p7, p17, p21)

Fixed by the brief: no horns, no demonic face, not cute, never a human-like Satan.

| Option | What is drawn | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Plain snake, posture carries "subtle"** | An ordinary flat-vector snake: smooth body, simple head in profile, one plain round eye, no fangs, no tongue emphasis. Subtlety comes from where it is and how it leans: close to her, between her and the fruit. | Truthful (the text calls it an animal of the field) and calm. Its identity (Rev 12:9) lives only in the text, so a child may read it as "just a snake". |
| **B. Attractive, patterned serpent** | Same build with bold, colourable markings (bands, diamonds) that make it appealing to look at. | Shows deception as attractiveness and gives children good regions to colour. Risk: "pretty" slides toward friendly or cute. |
| **C. Mostly hidden serpent** | Coils half-hidden among leaves, with only a small head showing. | The least frightening option, and it reads "subtle" as concealment. Risk: unclear to a 4-year-old. p21 must then show the full body for the first time. |
| **D. Pre-curse form (not yet on its belly)** | A serpent with a different posture or anatomy before 3:14. | The contrast with 3:14 is visible. Risk: the text doesn't describe its earlier form, so drawing one invents evidence (guardrails §4) and may look like a dragon or lizard. |

Cross-cutting choices under any option:
- Are the serpent's eyes shown? (Slit pupils read as menace.)
- Is the serpent on every first-couple page from p5 to p17, or only where the text names it?
- Does the teaching ever name Satan? That is a text matter for the author (see F18).

### D2 — The fruit (3:6; the tree "in the middle of the garden"; p3, p7, p9)

The text names no species. **Book Two p11 already shows apple and pear trees in Eden.**

| Option | What is drawn | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Generic round fruit** | Plain circles with one small leaf, no stem dimple or core shape. | Avoids a named species, but any round fruit with a leaf reads as an apple to a child. It sits close to Book Two's p11 apples, so the forbidden tree may look like one of Book Two's ordinary trees. |
| **B. Fruit kept small and unspecific** | The fruit is never shown close up; the tree carries many small fruits, and 3:6 is shown by the reach, not the fruit. | Invents nothing. Weak on 3:6 "a delight to the eyes". |
| **C. A distinctive invented fruit shape** | A shape no real fruit has. | Readers can identify the tree. But this is exactly what the brief says to avoid: it starts a new tradition. |
| **D. Same fruit as the trees around it** | The tree looks like the others (2:9: every tree "pleasant to the sight, and good for food"). It is identified by place (the middle) and by the serpent. | Theologically pointed: the sin is disobeying the word, not a special fruit. Consistent with Book Two's mixed-fruit garden. Risk: if its neighbours are apple trees, the apple tradition comes back in by the side door. |

Related: if Book Three avoids apples entirely, Book Two's p11 apple and pear trees stay as
"every tree good for food". No page in either book says what the forbidden fruit is. The two
trees in the middle (2:9) also raise how, or whether, the tree of life is drawn (F10).

### D3 — Clothing continuity (3:7 fig leaves; 3:21 garments of skin)

Book Two drew Adam and Eve in plain garments as a disclosed convention (its p33 footnote cites
Gen 2:25). In Book Three, 3:7, 3:10 and 3:11 turn on nakedness.

| Option | 3:7 is shown as | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Leaves added over the existing garments** | Fig-leaf aprons or belts drawn over the Book Two tunics. | Depicts the act the text names. It looks odd (clothes over clothes) and leans on the convention note to explain. |
| **B. Sewing shown as an action** | The couple holding large fig leaves, garments unchanged. | Shows the act without changing outfits. Holding is the pose most likely to make limbs thick (lesson from Book Two). |
| **C. Shame shown, leaves not worn** | Body language plus the fig tree (the current p11 baseline); garments unchanged until 3:21. | Avoids clothes over clothes, but leaves out the specific act of 3:7. 3:21 then has to look clearly different. |
| **D. Three-stage change** | p3–p9 in Book Two's garments; p11 onward in leaf garments that replace them; p29 in skins. | Tells the text's clothing story clearly. Risk: it implies the first garments were real clothes that the leaves replaced, which is the claim the convention note disclaims. |

Convention note: Book Two's footnote is in Book Two only. Book Three is read on its own, so
the question is whether it needs its own line. That follows the same reasoning as §6's
once-per-book Yahweh rule, but no rule here requires it; it is the editor's call. If yes, the
author writes it and it is registered `p33.footnote`. Interacts with F17 (printing "naked").

### D4 — 3:21: God clothing them (p29)

Fixed by the brief: the garments shown as a gift, with no divine actor and no animal's death.
WEB reads "garments of **animal** skins" (F5).

| Option | What is drawn | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The couple wearing the garments** | The new garments read as hide only by irregular edges; no fur texture (the coloring rules ban texture). | No actor and no death. That they are skins is barely visible. |
| **B. The garments laid out as a gift** | Two garments folded on a rock in morning light; the couple approaching from behind. | Reads clearly as given, not made by them. Risk: any light shape must not suggest a giver (§0). "Someone left them" is ambiguous, but that ambiguity is truthful. |
| **C. Their covering beside His** | The couple in new garments, with their dropped fig-leaf coverings on the ground beside them. | The strongest contrast between their covering and His provision. Depends on D3 having drawn leaves (A, B or D). |
| **D. Folded into p31** | No separate page; p31 shows them leaving, clothed. | Frees a page. Loses the gift moment the brief asks for. |

**Flag for Stage 2 (editor note, 2026-10-01):** the approved outfit vocabulary (sleeveless
tops, knee hems) may not fit garments of skin. Longer, heavier or sleeved garments are the
types the character bible marks FAIL or untested. Not solved here; test it at the style lock.

### D5 — Shame and hiding (3:7-10; p11, p13, and p15 if D6 approves it)

| Option | Device | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Body language only** | Turned away, heads lowered, apart, partly hidden. Faces are never seen anyway (DECISION-5). | Gentle and consistent with Books One and Two. Subtle for the youngest readers. |
| **B. Mood from the setting** | Dusk, wind lines in the trees, long shadows drawn only as outlines. | Mood reads quickly. Shadows must stay outlines (the no-solid-fill rule), and too much darkness becomes frightening. |
| **C. Peek-a-boo framing** | Peeking out from behind a trunk, a hiding posture children know from play. | Instantly recognisable and not scary. Risk: it reads as a game, which lightens the moment. |

A combination is possible. Separately, family-cast faces are visible. Options for a cast
child's face on a hiding page: a small worried mouth line, a neutral face, or the face turned
away. No tears drawn large in any option.

### D6 — Blame as a family-cast application (p15, p19)

The brief's frame: ordinary childhood wrongdoing (something broken, hidden, blamed), age-
appropriate, never humiliating.

| Option | Shape | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Two family pages, one scene** | p15: the pot is broken and hidden. p19: a parent crouches calmly; the child points elsewhere. Same outfits on both pages (bible v2.0). | Mirrors 3:7-13 step by step, and Book Two's text-then-you shape. A cast child is shown doing wrong across two pages. |
| **B. One family page (blame only)** | p15 returns to the first couple (3:10, "I was afraid"); p19 is the family page. | Lighter use of the cast, with the hiding kept in Eden. |
| **C. No family pages** | p15 and p19 become first-couple pages (3:10, 3:13). | No cast child is ever pictured as the wrongdoer. Breaks Book Two's pattern and leaves the song's "me and you" without a picture. |
| **D. The whole family shares it** | One mess, several people pointing at each other. | Nobody is singled out, and it matches "all of us". A busy scene risks cast-sheet bleed and pointing-hand problems (bible v2.2 allows one pointing gesture). |

Sub-rulings needed under A, B or D:
- **Which child?** Son (4, the readers' own age), Daughter 3 (6), or rotate across the
  series so no single child becomes "the naughty one".
- **Who gets blamed?** The dog (gentle, nobody hurt) or a sibling (truer to life, but it
  pictures sibling conflict).
- **Is resolution shown?** Confession and forgiveness (Ps 32:5; 1 John 1:9) on p19, or the
  page is left honest and unresolved and the answer waits (F16).

### D7 — The curse (3:16-19; p23)

Fixed by the brief: pain in childbirth is never depicted.

| Element | Options |
|---|---|
| Thorns and thistles (3:18) | Show on p23 · omit |
| Sweat (3:19) | A few small drop shapes (the common cartoon sign) · posture only (drops can read as tears or fear) · omit |
| Dust (3:19) | Bare dusty ground · dust on the man's hands · omit (D9 interacts) |
| 3:16a, pain in childbirth | Not depicted (fixed). Text: the author may address it, leave it unquoted, or leave it out of the book's printed scope |
| 3:16b, desire and rule | See F4 |
| The woman on p23 | Shown working alongside the man · absent from p23 · shown elsewhere only |

Trade-off overall: the fuller the curse page, the more honest it is to the text and the
song's "hurt lasts long", and the heavier it is for a 4-year-old. Thorns and hard ground are
concrete and easy to colour; sweat and dust are the least concrete.

### D8 — Cherubim and the flaming sword (3:24; p31)

| Option | What is drawn | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Gate and turning sword only** | A simple blade outline with flame shapes and turning motion lines at the garden's entrance; no figures. | Keeps Book Two's book-level "no angels, no wings" negative. A floating sword may read as magic. The text names cherubim, and the picture leaves them out. |
| **B. Wings without bodies** | Large wings flanking the entrance, echoing the ark's cherubim (Exod 25:20). | A biblical motif with no human-like form. Wings with no body can look eerie, and it needs a new style rule. |
| **C. Human-like winged figures** | The popular "angel" shape. | Readers recognise it. Not how Scripture describes cherubim (Ezek 10). A human figure barring the way may read as God or as Christ, which runs against the spirit of §0–§1. |
| **D. Distance** | The couple large in the foreground, walking away; the guarded entrance small and far behind. | Keeps the detail minimal, whichever of A or B is used. The guarding is implied more than shown. |
| **E. A sword of light** | A bright vertical blade shape. | Close to the text's flaming sword. Light must never be shaped into a figure (§0), so the outline has to read only as a blade. |

Ezekiel-style cherubim (four faces, wheels) are listed for completeness only: frightening at
ages 4–7, and impractical as line art.

### D9 — Death: "to dust you shall return" (3:19)

Never drawn under any option: a grave, skeleton, dead animal or coffin.

| Option | Where | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Text only, p23** | The teaching pointer sits with 3:19; the image shows dusty ground. | Honest to the verse; nothing to see. |
| **B. A gentle natural sign, p25** | A wilting flower or falling leaves through the window. | Gentle. Risk: death read as "things just wear out" rather than as the result of sin (Rom 5:12). |
| **C. Back matter only, p33** | Words for the adult reading with the child. | Protects the youngest readers. Delays something the song says plainly ("the hurt lasts long"). |
| **D. Named with "all of us", p27** | The pointer joins Rom 5:12 ("death through sin… passed to all men"). | Doctrinally exact; the heaviest option. |

### D10 — Hope without stealing Book Four

Fixed: no Gen 3:15 page. Guardrails §2 reserves **light, path and seed** as Book Four's
symbols for the Rescuer, so Book Three uses none of them as a hope sign. The brief allows one
closing pointer page facing Book Four, in the author's words.

| Option | How the last pages lean forward | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Hope from inside Genesis 3** | 3:20 (Eve named "mother of all the living": life goes on) and 3:21 (God clothes them: His care goes on) carry it on p29. p31 shows the exile plainly. p33's words do the leaning. | Textual and honest. Names no plan. |
| **B. p31 composition** | The couple leave together, not alone, under an open sky. | Hope through company and space. An open horizon can read as a path, so it needs a negative. |
| **C. A family-cast ending on p31** | e.g. the family at evening with a closed Bible holding a bookmark ("to be continued"). | A neat series device. The last image moves away from Genesis, and the exile goes unpictured unless D4 or D8 carry it. |
| **D. Cliffhanger** | The last image is the guarded way; p33 is the pointer page facing Book Four. | The strongest forward lean, from the song's "this isn't the end… God has a plan". The author must stop short of the 3:15 promise itself. |

Interacts with F13: the Track 3 teaching script already uses Book Four's title phrase.

## Further points needing a ruling

- **F1 — Gen 2:16-17.** Book Two D12 deferred the command and the tree of knowledge to
  "Book Three". This brief's scope starts at 3:1. p3 currently cites 2:9, 16-17. Rule them
  in (references and pictures only) or out. The teaching script ¶44 ("one simple rule")
  assumes the command is known.
- **F2 — Yahweh's first appearance.** 3:1 is the first verse in scope with "Yahweh". The
  author's Yahweh line is due on the first page that prints a WEB quotation containing the
  name. If 3:1 is quoted, that is p5. In 3:1 the narrator says "Yahweh God" but the serpent
  says only "God"; a pointer, if the author wants it.
- **F3 — 3:8 wording.** WEB reads "They heard Yahweh God's **voice walking** in the garden".
  The brief paraphrased it as "the sound of Yahweh God walking". A printed quotation would
  carry WEB's wording, which may puzzle a 4-year-old. Quote it, leave it unquoted, or
  paraphrase in the author's own words.
- **F4 — 3:16b** ("Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you"). It is in
  the brief's verse range. For ages 4–7: quote it, leave it unquoted, or address it in the
  back matter.
- **F5 — "animal skins" (3:21, WEB).** Quoting it puts an animal's death into print even
  though D4 doesn't draw one. Children may ask what happened to the animal.
- **F6 — 3:5 "you will be like God".** The lie offers what Book Two said they already were
  ("Made Like Him"). Pointer only, if the author wants the connection.
- **F7 — 3:22 "like one of us".** A plural, like Book Two's 1:26, which D9 left plain. Same
  question here.
- **F8 — Isa 53:6.** The second half ("Yahweh has laid on him the iniquity of us all") is
  substitution, which is Book Five and Six ground. p27 cites 53:6a only. Confirm.
- **F9 — 3:20, Eve named.** In scope but with no page of its own; folded into p29. It is the
  first time she is named; Book Two said "the woman". It may matter for D10 (A).
- **F10 — The tree of life (2:9; 3:22, 24).** Draw it on p3 or p31, or not at all? Book
  Eleven (Rev 22:2) brings it back, so whatever is drawn now sets its look for the series.
- **F11 — The 3:15 boundary on the curse pages.** p21 (3:14) and p23 must show nothing from
  3:15: no woman, heel, offspring or crushed head. Teaching pointers on p21 stop at 3:14.
- **F12 — Lyric "Me and you—we're all born wrong".** True to original sin (WCF 6.3; Ps 51:5).
  But "born wrong" could be heard by a child as "I was made wrong", against Book Two's "on
  purpose". It only matters if the author prints or echoes it.
- **F13 — Teaching script ¶50, "God didn't just walk away".** That is Book Four's title. An
  excerpt used in Book Three would use up Book Four's title phrase early.
- **F14 — Book Two's book-level style negatives.** "No serpent, no fruit being taken, no
  central tree, no angels" bound Book Two only. Book Three's style.md must reverse the first
  three on purpose and add its own. That happens at Stage 2, from these rulings.
- **F15 — "Subtle" vs "crafty".** WEB has "subtle". Many children's Bibles say "crafty" or
  "sneaky". A printed quotation keeps "subtle".
- **F16 — Confession and forgiveness (Ps 32:5; 1 John 1:9).** Both are on the brief's
  cross-reference list. Do they belong in Book Three as application, or wait (Book Seven,
  "Grace, Grace, Grace")? Placement affects D6 and D10.
- **F17 — Printing "naked"** (3:7, 10, 11). Depends on D3 and on which verses the author
  quotes.
- **F18 — Naming Satan.** Rev 12:9 identifies "the old serpent… the devil and Satan". The
  picture stays an animal under every D1 option. Whether the text names him is the author's
  call.
- **F19 — Who does the wrong.** Across the series, avoid one cast child repeatedly being the
  wrongdoer (D6). Book Two cast Son and Daughter 3 as sharers on p17.
